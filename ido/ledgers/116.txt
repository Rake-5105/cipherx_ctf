Sealed by the Chera house -- do not open without the king's signet
Filed by order of: The deputy scribe (under instruction of the senior, since retired)

Cargo recorded at the customs house but not declared to the public ledger:
- 18 maunds of pepper, re-routed past the royal wharf at midnight, three
  ships, the Yavana captains paid in gold not recorded in any other ledger.
- 4 sealed boxes of pearls from the Pandya prince's envoy, transferred
  direct to the inner treasury.
- 6 chests of frankincense, carried inland before dawn; broker unnamed.
- 1 ingot of Cypriot copper stamped with the Chera signet, held in the
  customs strongroom.

This ledger was filed in the regular sequence between the Tyndis silk factor
and the temple jeweller of Vañji. The deputy did not separate it. The senior
scribe, had he still been at his post, would have marked the seal as
royal -- and routed the file to a different room.

Visibility: sealed -- but the door is open, and the number on the slip is
all the gate asks for.

A note at the foot of this ledger, in the king's own hand:

"To the one who sees, let it be seen; to the one who asks, what was not
asked for shall be given."
